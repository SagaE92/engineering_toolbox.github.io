RC filter / snubber

τ = R C. First-order cutoff fc = 1 / (2 π R C). Stored energy E = ½ C V² at the voltage above.

Automotive notes

  • Input RC filter: often used on sensor or ECU analogue inputs to limit bandwidth and reject noise or spikes. Keep the capacitor close to the pin; check that the sensor can drive the chosen R. Set fc well above the wanted signal and well below the noise you need to reject.
  • RC snubber: an R–C across a relay coil, ignition coil or other inductive load (or across contacts) damps the turn-off spike and ringing. Choose a capacitor voltage rating above the peak you will see, including the inductive spike, which can be many times the supply. The resistor limits discharge current and damps the ringing.
  • These notes are qualitative. Component values and ratings depend on the load and must be verified for the application.

Fastener torque helper

  • Default preload = 75 % of proof load (editable). Proof load = stress area As × proof strength. As and proof stresses are ISO 898-1 values for metric coarse threads; A2-70 / A4-70 use the 0.2 % yield (450 MPa) from ISO 3506-1.
  • Torque T = K × F × d, with d the nominal diameter. K ≈ 0.20 dry, 0.15 oiled are typical starting points (editable) — actual K varies with finish, lubrication and tool.
  • The real target torque comes from the drawing or the fastener / manufacturer specification. This is a helper, not a substitute.

Strain gauge bridge

Vo/Vex = GF·ε / (4 + 2·GF·ε). The linear form is GF·ε/4.

Instrumentation notes

  • Outputs are small (often 0.5–10 mV/V). Use an instrumentation amplifier or a 24-bit bridge ADC (HX711, ADS1220 and similar), with ratiometric excitation (measure Vo relative to Vex) so supply drift largely cancels.
  • Quarter-bridge has no inherent temperature compensation: add a dummy gauge on the same material, unstrained (or in the Poisson position), in the adjacent arm. Half- and full-bridge bending layouts cancel uniform temperature and axial components if the gauges are well matched.
  • Keep leads short and of equal length, use a shielded twisted pair, and star-ground the shield at the amplifier. Constantan foil gauges are commonly 120 Ω or 350 Ω; 350 Ω reduces self-heating at a given excitation.
